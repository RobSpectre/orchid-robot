Running the Orchid installation

Two robot arms play the Orchid synthesizer, an AI musician (Hermes) writes the music, and a big screen shows it all. This page takes you from a switched-off table to a running jam.

Stopping the arms, fast. Turn the Orchid's big voicing dial any amount, or press Esc or Stop motion in orchid-robot. The arms freeze where they are. In an emergency, switch off the arms' power supply.

1 Inventory check

Before setting up, and again when packing up, count every item on the bill of goods and tick it off.

Ticks are remembered in this browser until you clear them. Anything missing or damaged: tell the project owner before the demo.

2 Before you start

  1. Check that both arms and the Orchid sit on their marks on the plate, and that the arms' rubber fingertips are on firmly.
  2. Plug the Orchid's USB cable into the laptop and switch the Orchid on.
  3. Plug in the arms' USB-C cables and switch on the arms' power. The arms stay limp until the software takes hold of them.
  4. Plug the PA into the laptop's headphone jack.
  5. Keep the arms' power switch within reach the whole time the arms are powered.

Plug in every cable before starting the software, and try not to touch them while it runs. Studio looks for the Orchid only when it starts, and plugging or unplugging a USB-C cable can lose the connection to the arms (see Lost the arms?).

3 Start everything

  1. Double-click Start Orchid on the desktop. A Terminal window opens.
  2. It checks the Orchid, the arms and the PA. If one is missing it tells you what to plug in: plug it in and press Return.
  3. It starts the programs and ticks each one off. Then three windows open: the visualizer, Orchid Studio and orchid-robot.
  4. Leave the Terminal window open (you can move it aside). Closing it stops everything.

Keep the orchid-robot window open. The arms only move while it's open. If it closes, they stop and hold where they are within five seconds.

The Terminal window also shows the PA's volume. The keyboard's volume keys don't work with this output, so set the level on the PA itself.

4 What's running

Four programs work together. You'll mostly use orchid-robot and the Hermes chat.

orchid-robot

Drives the two arms: connecting, calibrating, teaching them where each key is, and playing. It checks every move for safety.

5 Connect the arms

  1. In orchid-robot, press Find and connect all arms. It finds both arms and the leader by itself. Connecting only reads the motors: nothing moves.
  2. The sidebar now shows Keys Arm and Chord Arm. Click one to work with it. The sidebar steps (01 Connect, 02 Calibrate motors, 03 Train controls, 04 Correct keys) apply to the selected arm.
  3. If an arm shows as calibrated and its keys as taught, you can skip to the visualizer or a jam.

Many buttons that release or move the arms ask you to confirm first (Arm this step), and some count down five seconds so you can get both hands on the arm. Confirming alone never moves anything.

Lost the arms? Restart orchid-robot

Whenever a USB-C cable is plugged in or unplugged, orchid-robot can lose its connection to the arms: they show as disconnected, or Find and connect all arms doesn't find them. This happens from time to time, and the fix is to restart orchid-robot and connect again:

  1. Make sure both arms are resting or supported. Powered arms keep holding where they are while orchid-robot restarts.
  2. Check every USB-C cable is plugged in firmly at both ends.
  3. Click the Start Orchid Terminal window and press R. It restarts only orchid-robot; Studio and the visualizer keep running.
  4. When it says orchid-robot is running, click the orchid-robot window and press ⌘ R to reload it.
  5. Press Find and connect all arms.

6 Calibrate (only when needed)

Calibration teaches the software where each joint's middle and limits are. The arms keep their calibration, so you only need this for a new or repaired arm, or when orchid-robot asks for it. Calibrating means re-teaching that arm's keys afterwards.

  1. Select the arm in the sidebar, open 02 Calibrate motors and pick Follower (the arm that plays) or Leader (the arm you move by hand).
  2. Rest the arm clear of the Orchid and hold its weight: its motors are about to go limp. Confirm and begin.
  3. Midpoint: put every joint roughly in the middle of its travel, gripper half open, and hold still while it captures.
  4. Ranges: the 3D view highlights one joint at a time (base, shoulder, elbow, wrist bend, gripper). Move that joint gently to both ends of its comfortable travel, without forcing it, then press Save range & continue.
  5. Review and save. Then do the other arm if it also needs it.

Changed your mind half-way? Reset or reload calibration below the step starts again or puts back the last saved calibration.

7 Train keys and chords

Each arm learns a home (where it waits between notes), a rest (where it parks), and then one motion per control: the Keys Arm's 12 keys, and the Chord Arm's Dim, Min, Maj, Sus buttons. You teach by moving the leader arm; the robot arm copies it live.

Always teach Home and Rest first, on each arm, before any key or chord button. Every key starts and ends at home, and an arm may only move while the other one is parked at its home or rest. Without them, nothing has a safe place to return to.

  1. Select the arm in the sidebar and open 03 Train controls. A checklist shows what this arm still needs, with the next step highlighted: Home, then Rest, then the keys.
  2. Hold the leader roughly in the same pose as the arm, then press the highlighted button. The arm stiffens and starts following the leader. Hands off the arm itself from here.
  3. Home first: guide the arm to where it should wait between notes: above the Orchid but clear of it, and out of the other arm's way. Press ⌂ Home on the instrument map (or the highlighted Home step).
  4. Rest next: guide the arm to where it should park when it isn't playing, clear of the Orchid and the other arm. Press ☾ Rest, then Set rest here.
  5. Only now, the keys. Each key or button takes three captures, each with Space, so one hand can stay on the leader:
    • Hover: well above the key. Keep it high: in a song the arm glides from one key's hover to the next.
    • Touch: fingertip just touching the key, not pressing.
    • Press: press only until the Orchid sounds.
    A beep confirms each capture. After the press, the arm returns home by itself and the key is taught.
  6. Press Play to try it. With Studio running, orchid-robot shows what the Orchid heard, for example Orchid heard: C ✓, or Check the arm if it hit the wrong key or missed.
  7. orchid-robot moves to the next step on its own. Put the leader back near rest between keys, and keep going through the list.
  8. For the chord buttons, click Chord Arm in the sidebar. The leader switches over to it. Teach its home and rest first, then its buttons, the same way.

After teaching

  • 04 Correct keys → Test the path between keys plays every key in turn to check the arm clears the Orchid between them. The first run after re-teaching is deliberately very slow so you can stop it in time.
  • Arm speed and Press hardness in the top bar apply to every key. Try new keys at 1× speed first.
  • A key that keeps missing: select it and teach it again. Your old motion keeps working until the new press is captured, and ✕ Cancel re-teach keeps the old one.

If the arms, the Orchid or the plate get moved or bumped, taught keys can miss. Play a few keys at 1× or slower and re-teach any that miss.

8 Set up the visualizer

  1. Drag the visualizer window onto the big screen, click it, and press F for full screen.
  2. Move the mouse to show the control strip at the bottom: buttons for the four looks and four themes, and whether they rotate on their own. Keys work too: 1–4 for looks, Shift+1–4 for themes, D for a demo with nothing playing.
  3. Press S (or the cog in the top-right corner) to open Settings.

Showing your own videos

  1. In Settings, under videos, drop video files onto Drop video files here (or click choose files). mp4, mov, webm, m4v, ogv and mkv all work. They're copied into the visualizer's media folder (~/workspace/orchid-visualization/media).
  2. For each video, choose how often it plays (Every … min), whether it plays whole or only its first seconds, where to start, and its Sound level. Videos are silent by default, since the music comes from Studio.
  3. Under video schedule, switch on Play videos on their schedule, pick a transition (Iris, Crossfade or Glitch), and choose when videos may play: any time, only while Studio isn't playing, or only while it is.
  4. Press ▶ Play now on a video to show it straight away. V on the display stops the video on screen.

Changes save immediately and every open visualizer follows them.

Red full-screen alerts

When something disconnects, the screen turns red and says what's lost. Fix it, or, if you're running without that part on purpose, switch it off under Settings → connectivity alerts. Switch off WhatsApp, which this installation doesn't use. The status page lists every check with tips.

9 Start a jam

  1. Get both arms holding. In orchid-robot, select the Keys Arm and Play any key, then select the Chord Arm and play any chord button. Each arm now holds at home, ready.
  2. Check the visualizer's status page says all systems go (the WhatsApp line doesn't matter).
  3. Double-click Jam with Hermes on the desktop. A chat opens in Terminal.
  4. Ask for music in plain words, and press Return:
    • Play a 20-minute chill set
    • Play something dark and slow
    • Play a dnb set at 170 bpm
    Moods like dark, ambient or dnb set the start; chill and banger slow it down or speed it up; a number of bpm (60–180) sets the tempo.
  5. The drums and loops start in Studio, and the arms play on the beat. Hermes improvises a new song in each part of the set.
  6. Hermes sometimes asks what you think of what it's trying. Answer in your own words: it learns what this audience likes.
  7. Type stop to end the set. The arms go to rest and the music fades out over a few loops.

If Hermes says an arm needs attention (for example check the arm after a wrong key), look at orchid-robot: it says what happened. The music in Studio keeps going while you fix it.

After you stop the arms with the voicing dial, Hermes can't move them until you do something in orchid-robot (play a key, or send an arm home). That's on purpose.

10 Stopping safely

  1. In the Hermes chat, type stop and wait for the music to fade. Close the chat window.
  2. In orchid-robot, for each arm: Go to rest.
  3. With a hand under the arm, open Release or disconnect, confirm, and choose Disconnect. The arm goes limp, so support it as it settles.
  4. Close the Start Orchid Terminal window and choose Terminate. That stops Studio, orchid-robot and the visualizer.
  5. Switch off the arms' power, then the Orchid.

Closing the programs doesn't make the arms go limp: powered arms keep holding where they are. Always disconnect in orchid-robot, or switch off their power, with the arm supported.

11 If something goes wrong

No soundIs the PA plugged into the headphone jack and turned up? The Start Orchid window should say Sound goes to the PA. If it said the PA wasn't plugged in, close it and start again with the PA connected.
Studio doesn't react to the Orchid, or red "The Orchid" alertThe Orchid was plugged in after Studio started. Close the Start Orchid window (Terminate) and double-click Start Orchid again.
